Your Google advertising is one account covering all three Rasoi locations, with a separate campaign for each restaurant. That structure is right — it means Jersey City’s budget can never be spent showing ads to somebody searching in South Brunswick. This page walks through what is inside each one.

First, the mechanics

Your ads are assembled fresh for every search

This is the part most restaurant owners have never had explained, and it changes how you should read everything below.

You are not running a fixed advertisement. Each of your campaigns holds a pool of headlines and a pool of descriptions. When somebody searches, Google picks a few from each pool and builds an ad on the spot — so two people searching the same phrase can see different wording.

The practical consequence: every single line in those pools will eventually be shown to a real customer. There is no such thing as a spare line that sits unused. A weak or wrong headline is not diluted by the good ones around it — it gets its own turn in front of somebody deciding where to eat. That is why the wording review further down matters as much as the budget.

Both live locations are spending every dollar available to them. At $5 per day, a full month is about $150 — and Jersey City spent $153.19 while Iselin spent $153.02. Neither campaign is short of demand; they are short of budget. Every day, both restaurants go dark once the daily $5 is used up, and any searches after that point go to a competitor.

The two are earning their clicks differently, and it is worth knowing which is which. Jersey City is the volume location: it gets shown almost twice as often and buys clicks cheaply at 27¢. Iselin is the efficient one: it is shown far less, but 11 out of every 100 people who see it click, against 8 out of 100 in Jersey City. Iselin is the stronger performer on a smaller stage.

The actual wording

Every line currently in rotation

These are the exact headlines a customer can be shown today. Nothing here is a draft.

Seven of these twelve lines were pulled straight from your Google business listing, not written as advertising. “Suitable for comfort food” and “Suitable for unlimited food” are checkbox labels from a directory form. They are grammatically odd, they say nothing a hungry person cares about, and they are being shown to real customers in place of a line about your food. This is the single easiest quality improvement available on the account.

The Iselin ad was advertising Jersey City — this is now fixed. One of Iselin’s four descriptions read “Best Indian Food In The Jersey City Area” — roughly fifteen miles from the Iselin restaurant. Somebody in Iselin searching for dinner could be shown an ad naming a different town, then be sent to the Iselin ordering page. It was confusing at best, and it cost you clicks from people who assumed you were too far away.

Corrected on August 18, 2026, and re-approved by Google the same day. That line now reads “Best Indian Food In The Iselin Area.” Nothing else in the ad changed — the other three descriptions and all twelve headlines are exactly as they were. The table above shows the corrected wording.

The best-written ad in your account is the one that is switched off. South Brunswick names actual dishes — biryani, pakoras, the veg and non-veg lunch boxes — and carries a real 10% offer. That is exactly the kind of specific copy that outperforms “Dine-in available.” It has one flaw: “Rasoi direct10” is a discount code that was entered as a headline, so customers would see the raw code with no explanation of what it does.

Already decided

South Brunswick is paused because you asked us to pause it

To be clear about why one of your three restaurants is not advertising: you requested this on our July 29 call, for an August 1 effective date, and we switched the campaign off on August 7. It is not an oversight, and nothing is waiting on you.

It was the right call on the numbers. Its cost per click was $1.10 — three to four times what Jersey City and Iselin pay — and only 2 in 100 people who saw the ad clicked it, against 8 and 11 at the other two. In the thirty days covered here it spent $76 before stopping and produced no online orders. Left running for a full year it would have cost roughly $1,600 for nothing.

The campaign has not been deleted. The ad, the keywords and Google’s approval are all still in place, so it can be restarted whenever you want it. We are not proposing that in its current form — a general restaurant campaign is the wrong shape for a venue that mainly earns as a banquet hall. The better version is a catering-and-events campaign, which we owe you a proposal for.

Reading the numbers

Why the headline “conversions” number is not your order count

Google Ads reports a figure it calls “conversions” — 42 for Jersey City and 62 for Iselin over these 30 days. It is tempting to read those as orders. They are not, and the difference matters.

That figure adds together two quite different things: completed online orders, and taps on the phone number in your ad. Once separated, the same thirty days look like this:

LocationGoogle’s “conversions”Phone tapsActual online ordersOrder revenue
Jersey City42375$300.65

Both1049014$707.95

So the ads produced 14 online orders worth $707.95, alongside 90 phone taps. Google counts each phone tap as a conversion worth $1, which is why the headline number is seven times the order count. Phone taps are genuinely valuable — many of them will be real orders taken over the counter — but they are not measurable revenue, so we report them separately rather than folding them into a return figure.

One known limitation, and it runs in your favour. Our ordering pages do not always hand Google the tracking marker needed to connect an order back to the ad that produced it, so some genuine orders are never credited. Your true return is therefore likely somewhat better than $707.95 suggests. We are working on it and will tell you when it is fixed.
